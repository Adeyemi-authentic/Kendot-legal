Client Engagement Letter: Template and Fee Guidance

Reference: KL/INT/TMPL-004. Owner: Tomi Kendot (Managing Partner). Last reviewed: February 2026.
Internal document of Kendot Legal, a fictional firm. Demonstration content only. Not for clients.

When to send it

Send an engagement letter on every new matter BEFORE substantive work starts, and after the conflict check is cleared (KL/INT/PROC-005). No work starts on a new matter without a signed letter, except urgent court deadlines approved by a partner.

What the letter must cover

1. Who the client is (the exact company name from the CAC search, or the individual's full name).
2. The scope of the work, and what is NOT included.
3. The lawyers on the matter and the supervising partner.
4. Fees and billing (see below).
5. Who pays expenses such as filing fees and courier costs.
6. How we handle the client's personal data (point to the privacy notice).
7. How either side can end the engagement.
8. How to complain: first to the supervising partner, then to the Managing Partner.

Fee guidance (internal, do not copy into client letters without checking)

- Initial consultation: 50,000 naira for up to 45 minutes. Deducted from the first invoice if the client instructs us. This matches the fee published on the website.
- Hourly rates for 2026: Partners 120,000 naira, Senior Associates 80,000 naira, Associates 50,000 naira.
- Fixed fees are preferred for company incorporations, trademark filings and standard tenancy agreements. Ask the practice lead for the current fixed fee.
- Disputes: an estimate in stages (pre-action, pleadings, trial) with a fresh estimate if the matter changes.
- Retainers: monthly retainers for corporate clients need Managing Partner approval.

Payment terms

Invoices are due within 14 days. Matters with an unpaid invoice older than 60 days are reviewed at the monthly partners' meeting.

Common mistakes

- Sending the letter to the wrong entity name. Always use the name from the CAC search.
- Leaving out what is not included, which leads to fee disputes later.
