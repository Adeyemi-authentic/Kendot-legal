Rent receipts, deposits and unlawful eviction under the Lagos Tenancy Law

Landlords must issue detailed rent receipts and separate receipts for deposits and service charges, and self-help eviction is an offence.

Insight article by Bayo Adewale, published 20 May 2026. Practice area: Real Estate and Tenancy. Sources: Tenancy Law of Lagos State 2011, sections 5, 10 and 44.

Many tenancy disputes come down to one question: what can each side prove? The Tenancy Law of Lagos State 2011 requires landlords to give receipts, and it prohibits eviction without a court order.

Every rent payment needs a receipt

Under section 5(1), a landlord must issue a rent receipt every time a tenant pays rent. Under section 5(2), the receipt must state:

the date the rent was received;

the names and addresses of the landlord and the tenant;

a description and the location of the premises;

the amount paid; and

the period the payment covers.

Under section 5(3), a landlord who fails to issue a rent receipt is liable on conviction to a fine of 100,000 naira.

Deposits and service charges need a separate receipt

Some landlords collect more than rent: a security deposit for damage and repairs, payment for services and facilities, or service charges for shared areas. Under section 10, the landlord or agent must issue a separate receipt for these payments.

Self-help eviction is an offence

A landlord cannot lawfully remove a tenant by changing the locks, removing the roof, cutting off access or using threats. Under section 44, it is an offence to:

demolish, alter or modify a building in order to eject a tenant without the court's approval;

forcibly eject a tenant, or attempt to; or

threaten or molest a tenant, by action or words, in order to eject them.

The lawful route is a valid notice to quit (where one is required), then a seven-day notice of the owner's intention to recover possession, and then a court order.

Practical tips

Tenants: keep every receipt, and pay by bank transfer so there is a record.

Landlords: use a standard receipt that includes all five details listed above, and keep copies.
