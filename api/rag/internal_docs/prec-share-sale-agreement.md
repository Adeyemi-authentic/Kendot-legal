Share Sale Agreement: Precedent and Drafting Notes

Reference: KL/INT/PREC-001. Owner: Ifeanyi Obi (Partner, Corporate and Commercial). Last reviewed: March 2026.
Internal document of Kendot Legal, a fictional firm. Demonstration content only. Not for clients.

When to use this precedent

Use this precedent for the sale of all or most of the shares in a Nigerian private company limited by shares, where the buyer is a single company or individual. Do not use it for listed companies, for a sale of assets (use KL/INT/PREC-004, not yet written), or where there are several sellers with different warranty positions. Speak to Ifeanyi before adapting it for a foreign buyer.

Structure of the precedent

1. Definitions and interpretation.
2. Sale and purchase of the shares.
3. Consideration: the price, how it is paid and any retention.
4. Conditions: what must happen before completion (board approval, consents, regulatory approvals).
5. Completion: who delivers which documents on the completion day.
6. Warranties given by the seller (Schedule 3).
7. Limits on the seller's liability.
8. Restrictive covenants on the seller (non-compete and non-solicitation).
9. Confidentiality and announcements.
10. Governing law (Nigerian law) and dispute resolution (arbitration in Lagos).

Firm drafting positions

- Liability cap (clause 7.3): we act mostly for buyers. Our opening position is a cap equal to 100 percent of the consideration. Do not accept less than 50 percent without partner sign-off.
- Claims period (clause 7.4): opening position 24 months for general warranty claims. Tax warranty claims run for 6 years.
- De minimis (clause 7.5): individual claims under 500,000 naira are ignored, unless the client instructs otherwise.
- Retention (clause 3.4): hold back 10 percent of the price in an escrow account for 12 months where the due diligence report shows open tax or employment issues.
- Non-compete (clause 8.1): 2 years from completion, limited to Nigeria. Longer periods risk being unenforceable, so explain this to the client.

Completion checklist (Schedule 5)

- Signed share transfer forms for every share being sold.
- Board resolution approving the transfers and updating the register of members.
- Resignation letters from outgoing directors, where agreed.
- Filing of the change in shareholding with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) after completion. The associate on the matter diarises the filing on the completion day.
- Stamping of the transfer documents. Confirm the current stamp duty position with the Tax team (Halima Sani) on every deal, because the rules changed in 2025.

Common mistakes seen in past matters

- Forgetting to check the company's articles for pre-emption rights before signing.
- Leaving the warranty disclosure letter until the night before completion.
- Not diarising the CAC filing, which delays the buyer's control of the company's records.
